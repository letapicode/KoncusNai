# Dictation cancellation ownership: audit batch 3

Baseline: main `af4b6e6d7192776e42d17efac77228fd5d39438a` (PR #13).
Local branch: `codex/dictation-cancellation-ownership`. No push, PR, or merge.

## Confirmed defects and scope

- `DictationPipelineCoordinator` cleared/disposed its runtime source before accepted hotkey callbacks drained. Later stage lookups returned `CancellationToken.None`; providers ignoring cancellation could consequently proceed through transformation, insertion, history, and success. Ten initial behavioral regressions failed against main.
- Its started flag allowed repeated stop to finish early, and startup could register hotkeys after stop. Disposal could release the signal semaphore under accepted waiters.
- A saved hotkey delegate could acquire the replacement run. A separate red regression reproduced this after the first ownership change, before delegates were bound to their original run.
- `ChunkedTranscriptionSession.CompleteAsync` after disposal returned cleared results instead of rejecting admission. The red chunk regression failed; completion and disposal also lacked explicit lifetime ownership for completion registrations/results.
- `DictationRuntime` cleared service/coordinator references before awaiting stop. Canceling the caller's wait or a stop failure could escape drain/disposal ownership. Undo unregister failure skipped active undo draining. These paths share the service graph, so undo failure draining is a necessary adjacent fix.
- Partial startup made dictation ready before undo registration finished. Stop/dispose waiting behind startup's lifecycle lock left those hotkeys accepting; two further red cases confirmed this. Runtime now publishes an owned stop before waiting for that lock, cancels pending startup admission, and requests Core stop from the startup token callback without self-await. New start/restart callers wait outside the lock and recheck admission after acquisition; successful startup publication is atomic with stop/dispose admission.
- History caching/publication did not reject already-canceled or late successful writes. Only cancellation checks were added; cache-before-persistence, recovery classification, and retention policy are preserved.
- `WasapiAudioInputSource` treated a stop request as capture completion and could abandon timed-out native stop/dispose calls. The pinned [NAudio 2.2.1 source](https://github.com/naudio/NAudio/blob/v2.2.1/NAudio.Wasapi/WasapiCapture.cs) distinguishes `StopRecording` from the `RecordingStopped` acknowledgment. Finalization could run before a pending data callback finished. This directly blocked capture-worker draining, so the adapter now uses an internal `NativeCaptureLifetime`: shared stop, worker acknowledgment, and actual disposal completion. Old sender callbacks are rejected; replacement capture waits for previous cleanup. This is a necessary capture ownership dependency, not a new device/hotkey architecture.
- `WasapiAudioCaptureService` disposed buffers before input draining: a saved data callback reproduced `ObjectDisposedException`. A device error during startup could also be overwritten by late successful startup. Controlled red cases confirmed both. Disposal now closes admission, drains accepted start/stop operations and input, then releases buffers; late callbacks and late startup publication are rejected.

Inspected production paths include Core hotkey admission, capture/finalization, chunk queue/worker/completion, transformation/insertion/history, runtime start/restart/stop/dispose, undo drain, `ApplicationHost`/App quit requests, WASAPI and exclusive capture, Cohere/Python worker disposal, and insertion target/clipboard finalization. This is a lifecycle remediation, not an exhaustive re-audit of providers or other workflows.

## Decision record

**Classification:** architectural lifecycle coordination across Core/App; existing public contracts, dependencies, process rules, persisted data, and shutdown budgets stay unchanged.

**Decision:** keep a private per-run owner with one cached token, startup/callback leases, generation-bound event delegates, and shared stop completion. Publish closed admission and the stop promise under the ownership lock; unsubscribe and cancel outside it. Drain accepted users before capture discard, independent cleanup, source disposal, and final semaphore disposal. Restart waits for the previous teardown. Runtime retains its service graph until shared teardown finishes. Partial startup includes undo cleanup before service disposal.

**Alternatives considered:** caching tokens alone would not fix queued callbacks, startup, generation adoption, or source/semaphore disposal races. Clearing references and restoring them after failure would leave ownership absent during the dangerous interval. Giving up at a new Core timeout and disposing anyway would violate PR #13's retained-owner policy. A generic lifecycle framework or actor rewrite would enlarge the change without demonstrated need.

**Admission/commit ordering:** the run-lock check immediately before a stage is its admission point. Stop closes admission under that same lock before cancellation. If stop wins, that stage never starts; if the stage wins, its in-flight effects may finish. Post-await checks block the next stage even after uncooperative success. This does not make OS effects, cache stores, or filesystem writes atomic with cancellation. Cache and history have additional local token checks; already-admitted persistence/cache effects remain. Late success notifications are rejected. Expected runtime cancellation skips normal error/success UI. A late genuine provider fault is observed at the drain boundary; terminal reporting cannot escape an async-void adapter even if the diagnostic sink throws. General diagnostics behavior remains deferred.

**Ownership and failure:** caller cancellation abandons only its stop wait, including an already-canceled runtime wait. Repeated stop/dispose await shared teardown. Sources and completion registrations remain alive while providers/completers use them. Chunk disposal publishes closed admission before driving cleanup outside its lock, and completion commits under that same lock. Worker admission/results reject disposal and canceled provider success. Cancellation callback faults are aggregated only after draining, and independent capture/hotkey/overlay/session cleanup still runs. Faulted teardown blocks replacement rather than reviving a partially failed graph. Runtime's managed admission semaphore/sources are reclaimed with their owner instead of disposed under queued callers or cancellation snapshots; linked startup registrations are disposed.

**Normal release versus quit:** ordinary hold/toggle release still finalizes capture, transcribes chunks, transforms, inserts, and records history. Stop/quit may flush remaining capture with a cleanup token solely to discard it and release the device. It never transcribes that discarded tail. Queue/duration/character limits and target/secure-field safety remain unchanged. Clipboard restoration retains its existing `finally` and ownership checks; cancellation cannot undo typing/paste already dispatched or an already-created recovery copy.

**Self-await:** cancellation callbacks may request stop and return its shared task, but must not synchronously block on it or await teardown from the operation that teardown drains. The actual App/tray quit callback schedules `RequestShutdown` and returns; it does not await itself. No production dictation provider callback awaits runtime teardown. Arbitrary injected providers that await their own stop promise are uncooperative work, not preemptible cleanup.

**Bounds/tradeoffs:** no additional Core timeout detaches work. A stalled provider remains owned, preventing replacement and resource disposal. PR #13 keeps seven 10-second asynchronous stage budgets, truthful incomplete-cleanup reporting, and retained dependencies. It cannot preempt synchronous cancellation callbacks, a blocked dispatcher/native call, force termination, or power loss. This trades incomplete termination cleanup for avoiding use-after-dispose. No unrelated process is killed or adopted.

The WASAPI adapter's former 5/3-second abandoned waits are removed. Accepted native cleanup remains owned through acknowledgment and actual completion. Normal release may wait longer (inferred, not measured); a missing acknowledgment can stall recording until quit applies the outer timeout policy. Model/chunk duration and backlog limits are unchanged. Controlled native tests use no microphone, COM capture object, driver, or external process. A controlled fault reproducing request-only completion is rejected by the acknowledgment regression; this is not physical device fault injection. Seven new and 26 existing Audio cases pass.

**Compatibility/rollback/security:** no public signature, schema, project-reference, or package changes; no model/runtime installations. Audio grants its existing test consumer internal access to the controlled native lifetime. Release candidate generation showed only that friendship change, explicitly recorded in the compiled baseline. PR #11 selection, PR #12 ownership seams and urllib3 hashes/provenance, and PR #13 dispatcher/lifecycle-test isolation remain. A source revert restores the old race and is unsuitable as a correctness rollback; normal start/release/target recovery are regression-tested. Review belongs to Core/App lifecycle owners before any later merge.

## Limits and next work

Automated task barriers prove ownership while a provider is stalled and then released; they do not prove that an indefinitely stuck native worker eventually exits. Physical microphone, desktop focus/paste/clipboard, native model/GPU, logoff/session ending, clean installation, and other hardware are unexercised in this batch. No unlimited-recording claim is made. No new autosave guarantee is made. General hotkey startup/process adoption policies remain deferred; only current lifecycle admission and cancellation dependencies changed.

Next batch: isolate diagnostic sink failure. Acceptance: controlled failures in plain/structured/file/dispatcher sinks cannot interrupt product cleanup, suppress independent cleanup, change an otherwise successful operation, or escape event boundaries; original operational failures remain observable; reporting fallback is bounded, nonrecursive, privacy-safe, and does not turn expected cancellation into a success/failure notification. Preserve all lifecycle/process rules, add failure-injection tests, and rerun existing gates without weakening them.

Then: settings concurrency and autosave ownership; process adoption/hotkey startup; import bounds/cache retention; clean-install and integrity evidence. Release validation requires physical Windows user flows, long-recording/backlog boundaries, cancellation/restart/quit, focus/clipboard, session ending, prerequisite-free installation, and broader GPUs. Plan .NET migration as its own compatibility/packaging/CI batch. Optional performance experiments should measure startup, UI latency, worker/model reuse, disk/cache activity, memory/backlog, and CPU/Vulkan selection before proposing changes. None are implemented here.
